# M2: how Sanshodhan v0 gets built

Written 23 Nov 2026 from PLAN.md §5 (M2), EXPECTED_RESULTS.md and what M1 found (`notes/results.md`). PLAN.md says what M2 is for; this file says how: the data, the tasks with when each counts as done, the order, and what needs you. Like KICKOFF.md, it is a draft: object to any decision before T19 starts.

## Where M1 left things

| What M2 needs | What exists |
|---|---|
| An edit language with a schema for constrained decoding | `engine/ops.py` and `engine/ops.schema.json`: eight operations, targets cited like lawyers cite them, optional `every`, `last` and `to`; the executor checks every operation against the schema |
| An executor that refuses rather than guesses | The same, with error messages written to be sent back to a model. The audit of 24 Nov found wrong edits it applied without an error (a proviso's omission taking a sibling clause, letter ranges half applied); those are fixed and tested (`notes/audit-2026-11-24.md`, E1–E26), but only T25's verifier makes "applied" mean "right" where no consolidation exists |
| Before and after texts to check edits against | The IBC replayed 2016 to 2021 against IBBI's six consolidations; POCSO and the Arbitration Act against India Code's consolidations, on bases rebuilt from footnotes |
| A baseline to beat | `engine/baseline.py`. Held out, first reading, its **coverage** (operations produced and no instruction missed, not checked for correctness): 51% of the IBC's 2017–21 provisions, 79% of its 2026 Act, 93% of the 2019 Arbitration amendment. Its accuracy held out is mostly unmeasured: the one held-out Act later checked against a consolidation went from 14 of 15 covered to 10 of 14 applied cleanly, and one of the covered provisions deleted the wrong section |
| A comparison harness | `engine/reconstruct.py`: file-by-file word comparison, with differences the base text already had kept apart |

**What that means for M2.** The baseline already covers most drafting it hasn't seen (how much of that it gets right is E2's other half, measured per Act in T20), so the model's claim lives in the remaining 10–25%: "in the long line, the portion beginning with ... and ending with ...", "the opening portion", inserting a Part, words that occur twice, misquoted words, drafting that skips a level. Those are rare per Act, so a useful hard set needs many Acts: 100 hard provisions at a 15% hard rate is about 650 amending provisions, or 20 to 30 amending Acts. Getting those sources is the first risk of M2, not the model.

## Decisions this plan makes

Each one is a default. The ones marked **(changes PLAN)** depart from PLAN.md.

1. **The output is the JSON of `ops.schema.json`, unchanged.** One schema for the executor, the dataset and constrained decoding. New operations (inserting a Part, omitting a Schedule, "the long line") are added to the schema before T19's data freeze, never after.
2. **The input is the amending provision's text plus the provisions it can touch, each line prefixed with its ID.** The candidate files are the ones the provision names ("In section 30 ...") plus their neighbours, so a model can't target what it wasn't shown. The target field's `enum` is those IDs.
3. **IndiaAmend-200 is stratified, not all hard (changes PLAN M2.1):** 100 provisions the baseline misses or gets wrong, and 100 it gets right, drawn from amending Acts nobody has looked at while writing rules or training. The split gives the hard cases enough items to measure, but it is defined by the baseline, so the baseline scores 0% and 100% on its two halves by construction: those numbers are reported as such and never as a result. What the gate calls **non-formulaic** is the readers' label instead: each reader marks every item's drafting formulaic or not without seeing any system's output (decision 4), so the baseline's score on it means something. Whole-set numbers are reweighted to the held-out Acts' own rate of baseline failures (about 20%, not the sample's 50%).
4. **A label is gold when applying it reproduces the official text after the amendment, and a person has read it.** The executor's exact match is the first check; a reviewer reads every one in IndiaAmend-200 and 10% of the rest. Labels are drafted by the baseline or by Claude, never accepted unchecked. Because a label drafted by Claude could favour a Claude baseline where several operation sequences are correct, every system is scored on the law it produces (exact match of the after-text) rather than on matching the gold operations, and T26's frontier sample says which model drafted the labels.
5. **Splits are by amending Act.** Every provision of one Act lands in one split. IndiaAmend-200's Acts are chosen first and their IDs committed in `eval/indiaamend-200.json` with a hash of the items and the commit of `engine/baseline.py` that drew the hard half, before any training run. The baseline is scored at that commit, and no rule is added to it from a test Act afterwards.
6. **Silver comes only from checked replays.** A provision is silver when its operations applied and the files it touched are still there at the next consolidation and match it. Unchecked replays (the IBC's 2026 Act today) are not training data until a consolidation checks them; `model/examples.py` writes them to a separate `.unchecked.jsonl` so nothing trains on one by forgetting a flag. A provision not yet in force at a consolidation's date can't be checked by it and isn't silver.
7. **The base model is the newest ~1.5–2B open-weights instruct model when T27 starts** (Qwen3-1.7B is the reference point today), fine-tuned with LoRA first and fully if LoRA falls short. The 70B baseline is the newest open ~70B instruct model the DGX can serve.
8. **Everything stays local until T10's permission or the legal opinion,** except the model weights and code. IndiaAmend-200's text is Act text, so its public release waits for the same answer (PLAN §8).

## Gate (PLAN M2)

On IndiaAmend-200's non-formulaic items (the readers' label, decision 3): at least 15 points above the regex baseline, and equal to the 70B model or within 2 points of it at 5% of its cost or less. On the half the baseline fails by construction, the comparison that counts is with the 70B model. If it misses, EXPECTED_RESULTS.md's "If results miss" applies: the accuracy-against-cost curve across sizes is still the result.

## Risks particular to M2

| Risk | Early sign | Fallback |
|---|---|---|
| Too few hard Indian provisions | T20's Acts give fewer than 100 hard items | Shrink the hard half to what exists and say so; add Acts during M3 and re-freeze as IndiaAmend-v2 |
| UK drafting doesn't transfer | T27's UK-only model is far below the baseline on Indian items | Weight Indian silver and synthetic data up; report E7 as found |
| Gold labels depend on consolidations that are themselves wrong | A label fails only where the consolidation departs from the amending Act (IBBI's "any" for "an", the POCSO Act's "violance") | The amending Act's words win (decision of 21 Nov); the label follows the Act and the item records the departure |
| The 70B model needs more GPUs than the DGX gives | vLLM can't load it | A 4-bit build, or the largest model that fits, named as such |
