## Summary

The record-keeping is unusually honest: every result carries its caveats, and held-out numbers were written down before rules were added for an Act. What's published is right. Both law repositories match a fresh build exactly, and the live reader passed every M0 gate check that could be automated. M1's gate is met on its "every difference explained" branch, and T19 is done.

Below the published layer, the code has more silent errors than its tests can see. Three reviewers found 7 high-severity parser and rebuild bugs, 3 in the executor and baseline, and 1 in the reader, all confirmed. Each of them produces wrong text without an error. None changes the M0 results or the 74 silver examples. They do change the local Constitution history, two headline numbers in `results.md`, and how far the executor can be trusted as M2's judge. The evaluation design for M2 has two flaws to fix before IndiaAmend-200 is frozen.

## 2. Evaluation design

### 2.1 The IndiaAmend-200 gate is met by construction (high)

M2.md decision 3 makes the hard half "100 provisions the baseline misses or gets wrong" and the gate "at least 15 points above the regex baseline on the hard half". The baseline scores 0% on that half and 100% on the other by definition, so the gate asks the model for 15% on hard items, and the whole-set baseline is exactly 50%. Neither number says anything about the baseline.

The 100/100 split also misstates difficulty. Held out, the baseline produced complete operations for 51–93% of provisions, so a natural sample is about 80/20, not 50/50.

**Fix, smallest first:**
- Keep the stratification for power, but make the real gate the comparison with the 70B model on the hard half, and report the baseline's 0% as "by construction".
- Report whole-set numbers reweighted to the natural hard rate of the held-out Acts.
- Better: have the readers label each item's drafting pattern (formulaic or not) without seeing the baseline's output, and define "non-formulaic" from that label. Then the baseline's score on it means something.

### 2.2 Coverage is being read as accuracy (high)

`notes/results.md` is careful: "understood means the baseline produced operations and missed no instruction, not that they're right". M2.md's "Where M1 left things" drops the caveat: "Held out, first reading: 51% ..., 79% ..., 93% ...". The one held-out Act later checked against a consolidation fell from 14 of 15 understood to 10 of 14 applied cleanly, and one of the "understood" provisions deleted s. 26 of the principal Act. `observations.md` records three wrong edits that applied without an error in one day.

So E2's accuracy half doesn't exist yet on held-out data, and the IBC's 2026 Act (56 of 71 "applied") is unchecked.

**Fix:** reword M2.md's table as coverage; bring T25 (the verifier) forward, ahead of T20's new replays, so wrong-but-clean edits are caught where no consolidation exists; record E2 accuracy per Act as T20 replays them.

### 2.3 Claude-drafted labels and a Claude baseline (medium)

M2.md decision 4: labels are "drafted by the baseline or by Claude". If T26's frontier sample uses Claude, gold labels written in Claude's style favour it, especially on ambiguous drafting where several operation sequences are correct. Either draft gold labels without Claude, or use a different frontier model in T26, or score by after-text (exact match) rather than operation equality, and say which in the paper.

### 2.4 The baseline isn't frozen (medium)

Every Act read so far added rules after its first reading (the honest first-reading numbers are recorded, which is good). IndiaAmend-200 needs the same discipline: M2 decision 5 commits the items' IDs and hash, but not the baseline's version. Record the commit of `engine/baseline.py` used to select the hard half, score the baseline at that commit, and add no rules from test Acts afterwards.

### 2.5 Silver bases are partly circular (low, known)

POCSO's and the Arbitration Act's base texts are rebuilt from the same consolidation's footnotes the replay is then compared with. `results.md` says so, and the Gazette checks (95.5% and 94.6% of words in order) are the only independent evidence. Fine for training silver; it shouldn't be described as validating the bases.

### 2.6 Data volume (medium)

136 provisions from 8 amending Acts gave 74 silver (54%). T20's 20 Acts at about 30 provisions each give roughly 300–350 more, not the 1,000+ in M2.md's data table. Silver is also, by its definition, what the baseline already gets right, so it teaches the model the easy cases. The hard examples depend on readers who aren't arranged yet. Revise the silver target, and plan for UK and synthetic data to carry the volume, as the plan already allows.

Also: `out/examples/*.jsonl` holds all 124 applied provisions with a `silver` flag (74 true). A training script that forgets to filter trains on 50 unchecked examples. Write silver and unchecked to separate files, or have the loader assert the flag.

### 2.7 Two of M1's headline numbers can't fail (high)

- **"Replayed forwards, the history gives today's text: exactly"** (`results.md`, 22 Nov; README). Each commit in a rebuilt history stores the snapshot it was built from, so replaying the snapshots returns today's text whatever the undo steps did. The tests that assert it (`tests/test_parse.py:152,167`) are true by construction. They can't catch R1–R4 (section 3.3), which put clauses in the wrong order, drop a clause's text, commit 25 sections years late and date the 38th Amendment to 1979. The claim to make is narrower: the rebuilt 1950 Article 15 and Preamble match M0's hand-built ones.
- **E1, "483 / 483"**. E1 compares each parsed unit with its own segment of the source, so it can't see text put into the wrong article (art. 371-I inside 371H, R5), omitted articles written as live ones (131A, 226A, 228A, R6), or a footnote left in the body (R7). The file count, 471 articles, is wrong by those four. Restate both numbers after the fixes, and add one independent check: the article numbers found against the Constitution's own table of contents.

### 2.8 Commencement (low, known)

Consolidations follow commencement; replays follow enactment. The 2019 Arbitration Act's ss. 2, 3 and 14 aren't in force, so their files show as differences at the 2021 check and can't be silver. Until per-provision commencement exists, a check should skip provisions known not to be in force rather than count them against the replay.

## 3. Code: correctness

The pattern that matters most: **the executor's promise to refuse rather than guess has holes.** Several operations below apply without an error and change the wrong text. Today the consolidations catch these where they exist. In M2 they matter more: the model's output goes through the executor, T25's verifier doesn't exist yet, and T28's GRPO reward is "executes, passes the verifier, matches gold". An executor that accepts a wrong edit hands out reward for it.

The 74 silver examples are not affected. A silver example's edits must reproduce the consolidation, so a wrong edit can't be silver unless the consolidation has the same error, and the reviewer checked E1 against them directly. The M1 replay numbers stand.

## 5. Over-engineering

The code is already lean: about 2,500 lines of Python and 240 of JavaScript, two runtime dependencies (`pypdf`, `pyyaml`), no framework, no build step. What's left is duplication, not excess. Ranked by size of cut:

- `shrink:` The executor's hand-kept `OPS` table, the hand-written schema, and the test that keeps them in sync. Load `ops.schema.json` in `ops.py` and check each op's keys against it. One source, and it closes E10, E20 and E24. [engine/ops.py:15-24,239; tests/test_engine.py:118-120]
- `shrink:` `ORDINALS` is defined four times, in three cases. Keep one in `ops`, compared with `re.I`. [engine/ops.py:196; engine/baseline.py:29; model/examples.py:22; engine/constitution.py:34]
- `shrink:` `constitution.plausible()` and `ROMANS` duplicate `ops.follows()` and `ops.ROMAN`. Use one function. [engine/constitution.py:48,304-315; engine/ops.py:130-140]
- `shrink:` `" ".join(OMISSION.sub("", flat(x)).split())` is written six times; "strip markup, split words, append to differ" is written three times. One `words()` helper each. [engine/constitution.py:323,367,380,471,497; engine/rebuild.py:69; engine/reconstruct.py:103-117; tests/test_parse.py:326]
- `shrink:` `examples.py` applies each op list three times per record (`example()`, `in_view()`, round trip). Return the final law from `example()` and check in-view in the same loop. [model/examples.py:66-70,78-85,104]
- `shrink:` The "write a law dict to a folder" loop appears three times in the tests. One helper. [tests/test_parse.py:248-250,283-286,313-316]
- `shrink:` Three different tests of a file's kind (basename at two places, full path at a third). Use `file_id(path)` once; it also fixes E25's nested-Schedule mismatch. [engine/ops.py:243,252,268]
- `shrink:` The reader's `part()` and `heading()` fallbacks for folders without a README and files without a `# ` line. Contract 2 guarantees both. [reader/app.js:42-47,93]
- `shrink:` The numeral dict and `NTH` map the same thing. One dict. [engine/baseline.py:65,97]
- `shrink:` `KINDS` maps seven words to themselves. Keep the four abbreviations and use `KINDS.get(k, k)`. [ingest/footnotes.py:13-15]
- `delete:` `when()`'s second return value: both callers discard it. [engine/rebuild.py:248-255,289,294]
